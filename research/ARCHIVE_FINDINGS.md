## Summaries are useful leads, not authoritative character memory

The [CQ 252 record](https://airtable.com/appu1RWcXW9qFCJvi/tblVFIPpy8FQ4MfgM/viwOSHVSjI00NDOFQ/recB1REKVq6xLaFwm) contains a summary saying Tyler favors convenience and Johnny traditional controllers. In the attached transcript's listener question about an ideal retro setup, Johnny prioritizes ease, a large screen, and comfort; the other speaker explicitly says he is more of a stickler than Johnny. Johnny does express a controller preference, but turning that into a general traditional-versus-convenient split reverses the emphasis. Jen should not adopt that compressed characterization.

The [CQ 248 record](https://airtable.com/appu1RWcXW9qFCJvi/tblVFIPpy8FQ4MfgM/viwOSHVSjI00NDOFQ/recnJIQfIGmRVxjiW) summarizes the episode with broad Nintendo/Sega/PlayStation categories and lists several specific game titles. Literal searches in its attached transcript did not find “Panzer Dragoon,” “Shining Force,” “Metal Gear Solid,” “Final Fantasy VII,” or “Space Invaders.” Spelling errors and transcription omissions mean absence is not proof a title was never spoken. It is enough to withhold those summary claims until checked. By contrast, the timestamped opening clearly moves through CIB terminology, a concrete eBay search problem, and Tecmo inserts. [CQ 248, 4:51–13:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C0m5lrqLTy0&t=291s).

Practical rule: use summaries and tags to find an episode. Ground claims about what Johnny said, likes, owns, or believes in the actual passage and its date. Treat transcript spelling and speaker attribution as fallible, too. Neither a summary nor a historical podcast price establishes a current fact.
