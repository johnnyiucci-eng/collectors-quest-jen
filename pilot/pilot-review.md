# Ten-episode pilot: extraction and initial review

September 12, 2026. **Extraction complete; acceptance not established.**

The pilot now contains all ten selected episodes rather than only early material: 6, 7, 146, 200, 222, 248, 259, 263, 299 and 300. Total source volume is 254,821 words. The original archive is unchanged.

## Completed

- End-to-end reading and draft records for all ten episodes: 884 game/reference records and 514 lore items.
- Exact source/hash checks: 1,616 supporting excerpts checked after the initial review repairs, with no errors.
- Twelve retrieval behavior tests, including uncertain speakers, partial platform matches, inline corrections and the duplicate-upload count.
- Episode 299 title normalization, retaining unresolved names and candidate confidence; 215 short excerpts expanded across CQ 299 and 222.
- An internal middle-window omission check across all ten sources. All seven identifiable game references were already indexed at episode level, but only four had that exact sampled window linked. The other three now do. This tiny, non-independent sample does not establish 90% recall.
- Cross-episode links for the Portland report, Wii set completion and changing manual preferences.
- Comparison of the two CQ 263 caption bodies. They differ; both are retained and count as one episode. See [duplicate control](duplicate-control-263.md).
- A [cross-episode pre-show brief](brief-pilot-synthesis.md) with prior coverage, speaker distinctions, fresh angles and delivery cautions.

## What the pilot revealed

The local pipeline can retrieve useful evidence quickly: five different queries had median load/hash/index/search times of about 24–31 ms over five runs each. That is only local retrieval latency, not a model-response or full-archive extraction estimate.

Correction handling matters. For example, Johnny's Terror compilation anecdote ends with him retracting a missing-game suspicion, and Tyler's announced break has no agreed return timetable. A cohost that retrieves only the setup would give a materially wrong answer.

Lexical similarity alone is insufficient: a query involving Virtual Boy initially returned Game Boy Halloween material. Required-phrase and contextual-speaker filters now support narrower checks, but they do not prove all relationships within an answer. Returned excerpts still require reasoning and qualification.

One group-context error also surfaced in CQ 300: a Japanese-Pokémon auction description had been carried onto the following Left-Bros and Clu Clu Land entries. Those contexts were corrected, along with an incorrect N64 platform assigned to the Super Mario Kart comparison. These findings argue for a larger semantic review, not automatic acceptance after structural validation.

## Gates still open

- Full or adequately sampled independent omission review, including repeated mentions and minor game lists.
- Speaker/identity review and substantive claim-to-evidence review; 93 flags remain open.
- Adjudication of possible old-upload-only content in CQ 263. No automatic merge is justified.
- Independent answer keys and accuracy scoring. Development keys are drafts, not ground truth; reserved lookups did not produce a defensible accuracy percentage.
- Brief-usefulness and live-delivery checks.

Decision: keep the ten records as searchable drafts. Do not claim the 90% recall/answer targets, promote the records to accepted, activate the persona, or launch full-archive production on these results. The next work is acceptance review of this fixed sample, not more sampling or another clarification request.

All changes remain local and uncommitted. No external publication or synchronization was performed.
